# Intake checklist: what the recruiter needs to leave the call with

Use it two ways:

- **In prep, as the back-pocket list.** Mark each item answered by research ("confirm on the call") or open. Most open items get answered inside the hiring manager's replies to the main questions, so keep this list off the running order and resist turning it into questions one for one. Carry an item as a direct question only when it is a hard fact the search cannot start without (pay, location, process owners).
- **After the call, as the gap check.** The intake follow-up skill scores the intake notes against this same list, so the recruiter is prepared and checked against one standard. Every item still open becomes a [TO CONFIRM] with an owner and a date.

The same file sits in both skills' `references/` folders. Edit both together.

## The business
- The goal this year, in the hiring manager's words, and how it compares with last year
- What the product solves, who the customers are, and who buys
- Where growth is expected to come from, and the biggest risk to the plan

## The team
- What the team owns in that plan, and how it is measured
- What is working, and what is holding the team back
- How the team changes over the next 12 months, and who else is being hired
- Why this hire is happening now

## The role
- New role or replacement, and the story behind it
- Reporting line and team shape, with names
- What the person owns, and what success looks like at 6 and 12 months
- The day-to-day in concrete terms, and what sits outside the role
- The two decisions the search hangs on, resolved

## The ideal candidate
- Must-haves against nice-to-haves, each passed through the offer test ("would you sign the offer without it?")
- The background that looks right on paper and gets turned down, named
- Target companies worth sourcing from, and any to avoid (including Move clients and the client's own no-go list)
- Someone who has done the job well, and what made them great
- Where past candidates fell down, and at which stage
- Reactions to each calibration profile, and who else should review profiles

## Practical details
- Pay: the range, how far it can stretch, bonus or equity, who negotiates, and what can be said to candidates at first contact
- Location, office days, relocation and visa support, in words safe to share with candidates
- The interview process step by step, who runs each stage, and the typical timeline
- Where updates go (Slack channel, ATS) and who signs things off
- Company facts the client is happy for candidates to hear, and anything to keep private

## The pitch
- A candidate pitch built from the business, the team and the role, read back and agreed with the hiring manager

## Next steps
- Dates for profile feedback, the persona interview (booked, with a named person) and the start of outreach
