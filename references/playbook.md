# Intake playbook — the call

Read this before assembling a prep pack and before structuring any intake. It carries the anchors, the call structure (four pillars), the candidate-profile pillars, and the push-back triggers. The question set exists to fill `coverage-checklist.md`; this file is how the conversation actually runs.

## The three anchors

Identify the two or three decisions everything else hangs on, and place them above the question set so they frame the call. Anchors are role-specific but usually cluster around:

- **The shape of the role** — which of two talent pools (for example, hands-on specialist vs relationship-owning operator; analyst vs data scientist). "Equal parts X and Y" is unicorn framing; make them pick the anchor.
- **The true hard gate** — the single requirement that most widens or narrows the pool. Often a domain or a specific skill. Apply the offer test: would they sign the offer without it?
- **Seniority, geography and comp** — what "senior" means in real terms, where the role can sit, and the band against the market. Together these cap sourcing before it starts.

Resolve the anchors early. If the call runs short, these are what you leave with.

## Running the call — open, four pillars, close (60 min; compression rules below)

**The question set is a bank, not a script.** For each pillar the questions run broad to narrow: an opener that invites the HM to describe the topic in their own words, then narrowing follow-ups, then the gate-test that forces the decision. Carry more questions than the time allows and pick by the flow of the conversation. The **[must-hit]** questions are tagged in each pillar; when time compresses, those survive and the rest fall away.

### Open and frame (3 min)
State the goal: leave this call ready to source — requirements, calibration, outreach language, logistics — so sourcing launches in days. Name the structure and the recording.

### Pillar 1 — The role and how it runs (10 min)
How the role and the team actually run. Open wide, then narrow.

- **Opener:** "Walk me through why this role exists and what a great first year looks like." Let them tell the org story before you label it.
- Narrow to: business problem in one sentence in their words; new head or backfill and the org story behind it (a role split in two tells you more than a title); success at 6 and 12 months. **[must-hit: business problem verbatim]**
- Team shape and reporting line with names and sizes. **[must-hit]**
- **Relationship with the rest of the business:** who this role serves, who they answer to day to day, where their remit stops and adjacent functions begin, and the internal-service vs external-revenue split where it applies. **[must-hit]**
- The interview process step by step, with owners and typical timeline.

**Must leave with:** business problem verbatim · org story · named reporting line and team sizes · stakeholder interface · success definition · interview process + timeline.

### Pillar 2 — The candidate profile (15 min, the bulk)
This pillar is about the person, not the department. Work the candidate-profile pillars below, pre-loading a view from research and getting reaction on each. Open each sub-topic wide before narrowing to the gate.

**Must leave with:** the anchors resolved · hard gates vs preferences (offer-tested) · the identity axis stated in the client's words · the skills, behaviours and technical requirements named · leadership attributes if managing · the size and scale of org they come from · target-company reaction + do-not-contact list · geo and comp reality.

### Pillar 3 — Calibration, live (12 min)
Walk the starter set from the prep pack, profile by profile, on screen. For each: 30-second read, yes/no/maybe, and *why* in their words. The edge cases are the payload — each one was chosen to test a live question, so name the question as you show the profile. Capture: who else at the client should score profiles (the de-facto technical bar is often a named person, get them in the loop), where past candidates failed, the do-not-contact additions, and the 30-second yes vs the swipe-past.

**Must leave with:** a reaction per profile · the boundary each edge case tested, resolved or named as open · named scorer(s) · failure patterns from past candidates.

### Pillar 4 — Persona-lite (8 min)
The HM describes the best person they've seen do this work: what energised them, what made them leave or stay, the language they use about the craft. What would make this role a step up for that person. Who on the team is closest to the work — nominate the persona interviewee and ask for the intro in the room. Team environment truths (what it actually feels like), and what stops good people at competitor companies from moving.

**Must leave with:** motivators and frustrations in concrete terms · craft language worth quoting · persona interviewee nominated with intro agreed · honest work-environment picture.

### Close — logistics and sign-offs (10 min)
Geography and relocation stance. The comp band **and its phrasing rules**: what can be said in outreach vs at triage, what's gated until later (a band signed off for triage with agreed wording removes a week of Slack). Visa and relocation package, stated candidate-safe. ATS, booking links (get the actual URLs), comms channel, who signs off triage questions and how. Draft the three triage gating questions live and read them back. Company proof points the client is happy to see in outreach, and anything commercially sensitive that must stay out. Book the persona interview and set the calibration deadline before leaving. **Ways of working:** meeting cadence and how the HM wants to be updated, when they want to be introduced to candidates, and how quickly and in what format they will give feedback.

**Must leave with:** comp band + phrasing rules · booking link owner and URL (or named owner to chase) · triage questions drafted and read back · sensitive-topics list · ways of working (cadence, intro point, feedback speed and format) · next steps with dates.

### Compression rules
- **45 min:** halve the role brief and persona-lite; protect ICP, calibration, and the close.
- **30 min:** anchors, hard gates, calibration reactions on the top 4 profiles, comp phrasing, next steps. Everything else moves to async or the persona interview.

## Candidate-profile pillars (the core of Pillar 2)

Each pillar is a **bank, broad to narrow**: open with a describe-the-person prompt, narrow to the choice, then gate-test. Tagged **[must-hit]** questions survive compression.

**Example of the shift — from checklist to funnel:**
- Open: "Picture the person you would be thrilled to hire. Where have they spent their career and what do they do day to day?"
- Narrow: "An agency operator who has owned a P&L, or a brand-side leader who has built in-house teams?"
- Gate-test: "Would you sign the offer without agency P&L experience?"
The old narrow-first version ("Is having run an agency P&L a hard gate, or would a brand-side leader qualify?") skips the opening and gets a thinner answer.

**Shape and seniority**
- Open: "Describe the person doing this brilliantly a year in — what are they spending their days on?"
- Narrow: if you could only get one half — the deep specialist, or the operator who owns the relationship — which do you hire? **[must-hit]**
- What does "senior" mean here: solo-owning the work, or owning it with support? How many years, and is that a real bar or a proxy for depth?
- How hands-on is the day-to-day: producing the work, or directing and reviewing it?

**Skills and behaviours the person must have obtained**
- Open: "What has this person already done, in previous roles, that tells you they can do this?"
- Narrow to the specific skills already obtained versus the skills they will build here.
- Key behaviours: how they operate, not only what they know (bias to action, stakeholder management, ownership), anchored to a real example the HM has seen.

**Specific technical requirements and the domain gate (biggest lever on pool size)**
- Open: "What must they be able to do on day one that you will test in interview?"
- **Decompose any umbrella term.** Where the JD leans on an umbrella (digital, growth, full-stack, the marketing mix, AI-powered), break it into component disciplines and ask which are core, which they direct in others, and which are peripheral. (Marketing "digital" splits into programmatic, paid social, SEO, ABM, lifecycle, automation; do the same for any function.)
- Narrow: which skills are true gates versus strong preferences? Name each and apply the offer test. **[must-hit]**
- Is domain or industry experience a hard requirement, or does the underlying skill matter more? Where domain is soft, confirm it is a tiebreaker rather than a hidden gate. Name the wrong-domain explicitly (the regulated/adjacent trap: the industry that looks right on paper and gets rejected every time).
- How will the bar be tested in interview, and by whom? (Names the scorer for calibration.)

**Leadership attributes (where the role manages)**
- Open: "What kind of leader does this team need right now?"
- Narrow to team size and seniority managed, build vs run, and the leadership behaviours that matter here.
- Skip cleanly where the role carries no reports, and say so.

**Size and scale of organisation they come from**
- Open: "What kind of company shaped the person you want — where have they done their best work?"
- Narrow: stage and scale (startup build vs scale-up vs enterprise), and whether that is a real signal or a proxy.
- Lead with the tiered target-company hypothesis from research; get reaction tier by tier and a do-not-contact list. **[must-hit]**
- Name the wrong shapes explicitly so calibration slots are protected. Which adjacencies are worth one test profile before leaning on them?

**The gut test**
- Show me a profile you would say yes to in 30 seconds, and one you would swipe past. (The starter set makes this concrete.)
- What is the one question you wish recruiters asked before sending profiles?

## Dig harder — phrases that mean push

- "Preferably" on a domain or skill usually means required.
- A title that diverges from the responsibilities — pin the day-to-day.
- "Equal parts X and Y" — pick the anchor.
- Vague stakeholder or account answers — push for named titles and account sizes.
- "We're flexible / open" on comp or geography usually means undecided — get the real number or the real cascade.
- "Competitive salary" in the JD — get the band, the ceiling behaviour, and the phrasing rule for outreach.

## Principles

- Triangulate; push for the second answer with a different framing.
- Pre-load opinions and get reaction — research earns the right to propose, the call confirms.
- Read the responsibilities; resolve title mismatches in the room.
- Watch for unicorns.
- Surface where the bottleneck has been; it reveals hidden criteria.
- Reuse what we already hold; repeat-client context is an edge.
- Capture decisions in the client's words — outreach and screening quote the intake.
- Decompose umbrella terms. Any catch-all in the JD (digital, growth, full-stack, the marketing mix, AI-powered) breaks into component disciplines; ask which are core and which the person directs in others. This holds for every function.

## Next steps after the call

1. Log the intake (Moment 3): intake notes document, gap check.
2. Profile calibration completed within 24 hours: the starter set plus refinements, scored 1–5 by the named scorer(s).
3. Persona interview **if the gap check calls for it** (20–25 min with the nominated person), for outreach language.
4. Triage gating questions signed off through the agreed channel.
5. Launch outreach within 48–72 business hours of sign-off.
