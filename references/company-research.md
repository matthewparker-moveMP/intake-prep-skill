# Company research — the deep external run

Deep external research on the client: the company, its people, funding, growth, and competitors. This is the "online" pass in PREP, run properly. It earns the right to propose in the room (pre-loaded opinions beat blank questions), it stocks the talent-reality section of the prep pack, and it seeds the company proof points and the why-now story outreach draws on.

**Run it as its own research pass.** The work is broad and context-heavy, so treat it as a self-contained agent: fan out across the dimensions below, pull primary sources, verify, and return a tight research brief rather than a pile of tabs. Where the harness allows, delegate it to a dedicated research subagent (or the standalone `deep-research` skill if present) so the main PREP context stays clean and the dimensions run in parallel. It also runs inline when no subagent is available.

Internal documentation still comes first (PREP research order): a repeat-client profile can settle in one line what an hour of web research would guess at. This run fills the external picture and pressure-tests what the internal docs claim.

## The dimensions

Cover all seven. Each lists what to find, where to look, and what it feeds.

**1. Company profile and business model**
- What they do, who they sell to, how they make money, stage and size, HQ and office footprint.
- Sources: company site and product pages, the newsroom, LinkedIn company page, Crunchbase/Dealroom overview.
- Feeds: the role decoded, and the locked company facts for outreach.

**2. Funding and financials**
- Total raised, last round (stage, amount, date, lead investors), valuation where public, runway signals, revenue or ARR where disclosed, profitability posture.
- Sources: Crunchbase, Dealroom, PitchBook, TechCrunch/Sifted/press releases, the company blog's funding announcements, SEC/Companies House filings for later-stage or public firms.
- Feeds: why-now messaging (a fresh raise is a growth story), comp-band plausibility, and the proof points a candidate cares about (backing, stability).

**3. Growth signals**
- Headcount trend and hiring velocity (are they scaling, holding, or contracting), team the role sits in and how fast it is growing, product launches, market or geographic expansion, web-traffic trajectory, notable customers.
- Sources: LinkedIn company page (headcount over time, open roles), the careers page volume, SimilarWeb for traffic, product and launch news, G2/Capterra momentum.
- Feeds: talent reality (is this a build or a hold), the pace the candidate is signing up for, and the honest work-environment picture.

**4. People and leadership**
- Founders and their background, the exec team, the hiring manager and their tenure and prior companies, recent senior hires and senior departures, the board and lead investors.
- Sources: LinkedIn (company page and individual profiles, via the browser while logged in), the company's team/about page, funding-announcement quotes, press.
- Feeds: intake rapport and the who-you-answer-to picture, persona-interview target selection, and read-across to the group structure (hand to `people-search.md`).

**5. Competitors and market position**
- Direct competitors and category, where this company wins and loses, market momentum and any consolidation, the talent-competitor pool (who they hire from and lose to).
- Sources: Crunchbase/Dealroom "similar companies", analyst and category write-ups, review-site comparison pages, the company's own positioning, competitor career pages for the same role.
- Feeds: target-company tiering (the ICP hypothesis to react to on the call), the anti-profile, and the competitive angle in outreach.

**6. Employer brand and culture**
- What it is like to work there, glassdoor rating and themes, public engineering/marketing brand, remote/hybrid stance, any reputational or trust signals worth knowing.
- Sources: Glassdoor, Blind, the company blog and social, press on culture, employee posts.
- Feeds: the honest work-environment picture, the anti-sells outreach must route around, and messaging tone.

**7. Recent moves and news (last 6 to 12 months)**
- Raises, launches, pivots, acquisitions, layoffs, leadership changes, awards, controversies. A dated timeline of the moves that matter.
- Sources: news search, the newsroom, LinkedIn posts, funding databases.
- Feeds: why the role exists now, the why-now hook, and anything commercially sensitive to keep out of outreach.

## Source map and tool priority

1. **Internal docs (whatever is connected: Notion, Drive, account notes)** — first, always. Verified and specific.
2. **Web search and fetch** — the backbone. Broad landscape query per dimension, then fetch the primary source (the funding announcement itself, the filing, the company page) rather than trusting an aggregator's summary.
3. **Funding databases** — Crunchbase, Dealroom, PitchBook via the browser or web fetch for rounds, investors, and similar-company lists.
4. **SimilarWeb connector** — traffic trajectory and channel mix where connected.
5. **People-data connectors (Apollo, ZoomInfo, Clay)** — headcount, org, and named leadership at scale where connected.
6. **LinkedIn via the browser (Claude in Chrome)** — company page for headcount trend and open roles; individual profiles for leadership background. Mind rate limits; pull a focused set.
7. **Review sites** — Glassdoor, Blind, G2, Capterra for culture and product momentum.

Several connectors (SimilarWeb, Apollo, ZoomInfo, Crunchbase pro) may need authorisation. Where one is absent, say so and name what it would have added, rather than guessing the number.

## Method

- **Broad to deep.** Open each dimension with a landscape sweep, then narrow to the primary source and pull the specific fact. Depth on funding, growth, and people; a lighter touch on the rest unless the role demands it.
- **Fan out.** The dimensions are independent; research them in parallel where the harness allows, then synthesise.
- **Triangulate the load-bearing numbers.** A funding figure or headcount that will shape comp or messaging gets a second source before it is stated as fact.
- **Recency wins.** Prefer the last 12 months; date every moving number and flag anything older than a year as possibly stale.

## Verification and integrity

- **Cite every fact** with source and date. A claim without a source is a hypothesis, and gets marked as one.
- **Real sources only.** State funding, headcount, and valuation as found, with the link. Never invent or round-up a number to make a story cleaner.
- **Separate confirmed from rumoured.** Announced round versus press speculation; disclosed revenue versus estimate. Say which.
- **Name the gaps.** Where a figure is private or a connector is missing, say "not disclosed" or "would need [tool]", not a guess.

## Delivery

Return a **company research brief**: a short confirmed-facts block (business model, funding, headcount, HQ, key people) that is sign-off-safe for outreach, then a dimension-by-dimension synthesis with sources, then a dated recent-moves timeline, and finally the open questions and hypotheses to test on the call. It flows into the prep pack: the confirmed facts and moves under "what we already know" and "talent reality", the why-now and proof points staged for outreach, and the competitor tiering into the target-company hypothesis the HM reacts to.
