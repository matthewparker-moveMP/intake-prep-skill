# The recruiter screen

The recruiter's guide for the live first-round call: 30 minutes, about three pages. It is separate from any digital yes-or-no triage the client or the sourcing engine runs. The reference is a Move screen for a Partnerships Manager search, cut down for a first call.

## The quality bar

- **A few killer questions.** Each one separates strong from weak candidates quickly. Cut the easy, surface-level questions that every candidate passes.
- **Nothing the CV already answers.** Call time goes on what paper can't show. Facts from the CV and portfolio are checked before the call.
- **Built from what the hiring manager pushed hardest on.** Use the summary's non-negotiables, top 3 differentiators, "what each stage assesses" and "what causes a no". Quote the hiring manager where it sharpens the point.
- **Evidence over opinion.** Every killer question asks for a real example and pushes for numbers the candidate clearly owns, then sense-checks them (Move's own Talent Partner screen does this with hire rates: 30 to 40% a month is believable; far higher on a big load usually means inbound).
- **A "why we ask" line on every question,** so the recruiter knows what each one is for and can adapt it live.
- **Fewer, sharper questions beat a long list.** Three or four killer questions. Five only when the hiring manager named five things with equal force.

## The template

**When the template check found the client's own screening template, interview kit or scorecard** for this stage, use its structure and wording, and put the basics and the killer questions inside it. Keep the quality bar above either way.

Otherwise, build with `scripts/build_docs.js`, `"layout": "doc"`, `"size": 19`. Title: `Recruiter Screen - [Client] - [Role]`.

**Header table** (two columns): Client · Role · Stage ("Move screen, before anything goes to [client]") · Owner (the recruiter) · Duration ("30 minutes: 10 basics, 15 role, 5 sell and close") · Built from (the intake date, the HM summary, the JD, any calibration feedback).

**Read this first** (a callout): the two or three things that decide this screen, in one or two sentences. Where the intake showed that decision-makers weight things differently, say who is the harder marker and screen to their bar.

**1. The basics, every call (10 minutes).** A table: Topic · How to ask · Listen for. Same topics on every screen, worded for this role:

| Topic | How to ask | Listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why now | "What has you open to a conversation at the moment?" | A pull toward something specific, or only a push away from the current job |
| What next | "What do you want your next role to give you that this one doesn't?" | Whether it matches what this role offers (use the summary's "why a strong candidate would move") |
| What would keep them | "What would make you stay where you are?" | Counter-offer risk, and what to address in the sell |
| Pay | "What base are you working to?" Then the band in the agreed wording | Inside or close to the band. Note it and pass it up: Move never declines on pay alone, that call sits with the hiring manager |
| Notice and start | "What's your notice period, and when could you start?" | Record it |
| Right to work and location | "Where are you based, and do you have the right to work there?" Add the office pattern or eligible countries | Pass or stop against the agreed locations |
| Other processes and availability | "Are you in other processes, and how far along? When could you interview in the next two weeks?" | Timeline pressure, and slots for the first interview |

**2. The role screen (15 minutes).**

*Check before the call first:* one short paragraph listing the hard requirements the CV and portfolio already answer (employers, titles, team size with dates, training, location). The recruiter checks these before dialling and confirms one on the call only when the CV leaves it unclear. They take no call time.

*Then the gates: what the CV can't tell you.* A table: Gate · How to ask · Pass · Stop. Three or four gates, drawn from the non-negotiables. A gate earns its place only when the answer is invisible on paper. Each one is one of these:

- **A live test:** the candidate does the thing on the call. Partnerships: switch into German for two minutes and hold a partner-style conversation. Art Director: "explain why this piece looks the way it does, as if I were the CEO and I didn't like it."
- **A reaction test:** say the uncomfortable truth and watch the response. Partnerships: "The first months are two to twenty partner conversations a day, mostly cold. Does that sound like the job you want?"
- **A speed test:** a question a real practitioner answers instantly ("what are you proudest of?", "your three favourite brands?").
- **A recency test:** "What did you use [the skill] for this week?" A claim on a CV is easy; last Tuesday's example is hard to invent.
- **The story behind a CV line:** "Talk me through this period" for a gap or a short stint.

A question whose answer sits on the CV ("how many people have you managed?", "where did you study?") belongs in the check before the call.

*The hiring manager's own questions,* when they named any on the call ("ask them what they're proudest of"): use them word for word. Most make good speed or live tests, so they usually sit in the gates table with a quote from the hiring manager.

*Then three or four killer questions,* one per thing the hiring manager pushed hardest on. For each:

- **Sub-heading:** the dimension, and its weight when the intake made it clear ("Hunting, evidenced. Weight: highest.")
- **Why we ask:** one line tying it to the hiring manager's words
- **Lead question:** open, example-based ("Walk me through the last partner you opened completely from cold. Start at how you found them and take me to signature.")
- **Follow-ups:** three or four short probes, including one that asks for numbers
- **Sounds strong / sounds weak:** a two-column table, three rows

**3. Sell and close (5 minutes).**

- **The hard parts, said out loud:** a table, Say this · Good reaction · Bad reaction, two or three rows, from the summary's challenges. A candidate who flinches here declines later.
- **The sell:** four or five bullets from "why a strong candidate would move" and the pitch the hiring manager agreed. Everything flagged "Not for candidates" stays out.
- **Close:** confirm notice, pay and interview availability, and explain the client's process stage by stage. Link the live posting (or the careers page) to send after the call.

**4. After the call.**

- **Scorecard:** a table, one row per killer question: Dimension · 1 to 2 · 3 · 4 to 5, each cell describing what that score sounds like.
- **Decision rule:** when to submit (for example, 4 or above on the top dimension and 3 or above on the rest, with every gate passed), and when to hold and discuss.
- **Instant no's:** four to six bullets, from "backgrounds that struggle" and "what causes a no even when strong".
- **A note that the scorecard is a first draft:** straight after the intake it rests on the hiring manager's words. Tighten the anchors and the decision rule once calibration feedback comes back.

## Length

About three pages, with the part used live on the call (sections 1 to 3) first. Three rows per strong and weak table, three or four follow-ups per question. When it runs long, cut table rows first, then follow-ups. Keep every killer question and every gate.
